# ONCOGUIDE 1.3.4 — Marker and pathology guidance

This file documents the purpose and limitations of the pathway-specific tumour-marker, histopathology/IHC and molecular-testing suggestions shown in the report.

## How the application uses these suggestions

- A pathway must first activate from the recorded symptom/history rules before its pathway-specific suggestions are displayed.
- The general risk-factor burden index and pathway assessment-priority index are experimental, non-predictive indices. They do not independently order or mandate tests.
- Low index with no concerning symptoms: no automatic cancer-specific serum-marker panel.
- Intermediate index or relevant persistent symptoms: consider targeted initial evaluation based on symptoms, objective findings and the activated pathway.
- High index or multiple concerning findings: prioritise clinician review and pathway-specific evaluation; urgent safety flags always take precedence.
- Suspicious examination/imaging or an abnormal initial investigation may lead the treating team to consider tissue sampling and histopathology. IHC and molecular tests are selected after review of morphology, site, differential diagnosis, stage and intended clinical use.

## Important interpretation safeguards

1. Tumour markers are not interchangeable and are often not suitable for screening. A raised result does not establish cancer, and a normal result does not reliably exclude it.
2. IHC examples in the application are illustrative differential-diagnosis or predictive-biomarker considerations, not a universal panel. A qualified pathologist selects the actual panel.
3. Some biomarkers (for example, ER/PR and HER2 in confirmed breast cancer) have predictive/treatment-planning roles; that does not make them general screening tests.
4. Paediatric tumour markers and IHC are especially tumour-specific. Urgent paediatric assessment must not be delayed while awaiting markers.
5. Local availability, laboratory validation, national guidance and current clinical standards must be checked by the treating team.
6. This package is a software presentation prototype, not a clinical decision-support device validated for patient care. All suggestions require specialist review before any clinical use.

## Reference starting points for clinical review

- US National Cancer Institute (NCI), *Tumor Markers*: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/diagnosis-staging/diagnosis/tumor-markers-fact-sheet
- College of American Pathologists (CAP), *Estrogen and Progesterone Receptor Testing in Breast Cancer Guideline*: https://www.cap.org/protocols-and-guidelines/cap-guidelines/current-cap-guidelines/guideline-recommendations-for-immunohistochemical-testing-of-estrogen-and-progesterone-receptors-in-breast-cancer
- CAP / ASCO, *HER2 Testing in Breast Cancer — 2023 Guideline Update*: https://www.cap.org/cap-guidelines/her2-testing-in-breast-cancer-2023-guideline-update
- CAP, *Current CAP Guidelines* (includes laboratory workup and molecular biomarker guidelines): https://www.cap.org/protocols-and-guidelines/cap-guidelines/current-cap-guidelines/

These references are starting points for the clinical validation process, not proof that every listed marker is appropriate for every patient or tumour.
